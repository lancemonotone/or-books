Hi Antara and Colin,

Thanks for raising this, and I'm really glad to hear that you're both happy with the work so far. I completely understand the need to keep a close eye on the budget, and I want to explain how I approach estimated hours.

My intention is to estimate as closely as possible the initial time required to complete each task, based on the scope as I understand it at the time. The estimate covers the time I anticipate will be needed for the initial implementation. However, unforeseen difficulties or rounds of revisions can require additional time.

Some of the initial cost reflects the 8.5 hours of work during the Discovery wave: auditing the site and syncing the staging environment because BigCommerce couldn't provide a clean sandbox. That foundational work was necessary to support everything that followed and won't be billed again for future waves that depend on it. I also included the global removal and replacement of Helvetica, which wasn't part of the original project.

I want to clarify that the changes resulting from the August feedback that simply reverted something to its previous state were straightforward and weren't charged to the project. The additional hours were primarily related to the implementation work required by the other changes. As I got into the code, as I noted in Task 2.6, I found that many pages in the theme seemed to have been added ad hoc over time without following a standard design system that shared HTML structure and styles across the site. Because of this, seemingly simple design tweaks often required underlying structural work to make them work consistently on every page.

Rather than continuing to address these issues page by page, I used the opportunity to standardize components into global styles and templates, giving us a significant head start on the rest of the project. As such, many of the tasks in later waves will now require substantially less time than originally estimated. For example, standardizing the buttons and sliders means Tasks 4.5 and 4.8 in Wave 2 are already essentially finished.

I've been logging my time in Briefboard as the work progresses, including before the August feedback round, so my actual hours have been visible throughout the process. That said, I realize I shouldn't have relied on Briefboard alone. I should have flagged when individual tasks were starting to accumulate additional hours during the feedback process and checked in with you before continuing. Without that checkpoint, I understand that it might have been difficult to notice when additional hours were adding up.

Going forward, I suggest we put a hard checkpoint in place for the next waves. If a task is getting close to its estimate and looks like more implementation work or another round of revisions will be needed, I'll stop and let you know. I'll explain what's left to do and give you an estimate for the additional time, and you can decide whether you'd like me to continue.

I think that will give you much better visibility into the budget while still giving us some flexibility when a task turns out to be more involved than it initially appeared. It should also make it easier to prioritize the most important improvements within the budget available each month.

We have already significantly improved the site's accessibility, readability, and overall aesthetics, and I want everyone to be happy with the results of our collaboration. Revisions do take time and can add to the project cost, but assuming the remaining feedback is relatively minor, I'll finish the Wave 1 updates at no additional charge so we can close out this first wave and launch to the live site.

Please let me know if this approach works for you?

Thank you,
